Gavin Lowe

Lowe’s attack on a well-known authentication protocol showed why protocol designs require adversarial analysis, not just intuitive arguments about cryptographic primitives.

Gavin Lowe is a computer scientist associated with the University of Oxford whose work includes formal methods and security-protocol analysis.

An attack that changed a protocol

In 1995, Lowe demonstrated a man-in-the-middle attack on the Needham–Schroeder public-key protocol using formal methods. The corrected variant is commonly known as the Needham–Schroeder–Lowe protocol.

Why the result matters

The episode became a durable lesson: sound cryptographic building blocks do not automatically yield a sound protocol. Formal modeling can reveal attacks that informal review misses.
