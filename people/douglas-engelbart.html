Douglas Engelbart

Engelbart's systems research anticipated many practices of collaborative computing. His 1968 demonstration is significant to technical culture because it showed interacting ideas—pointing devices, shared work, links, and networked collaboration—as a coherent system.

Douglas Carl Engelbart was an American engineer and researcher who pursued computers as tools for augmenting human collaboration. At SRI, he founded the Augmentation Research Center and developed the oN-Line System, or NLS.

The 1968 demonstration

On 9 December 1968, Engelbart and colleagues demonstrated NLS in San Francisco. The presentation included a pointing device later known as the mouse, hypertext, shared-screen collaboration, and interactive editing.

In memoriam

Engelbart died in 2013. His institute and archival institutions continue to preserve a record of a career focused on collective problem-solving through computing.
