John McCarthy

McCarthy’s work helped make computers interactive, programmable environments rather than isolated batch calculators. Those environments were central to the hacker cultures that formed around MIT and Stanford laboratories.

John McCarthy was an American computer scientist whose work spanned artificial intelligence, programming languages, and interactive systems. He coined the term “artificial intelligence” in a 1955 Dartmouth proposal and developed Lisp at MIT.

Interactive computing culture

McCarthy advocated time-sharing, allowing many people to interact with a costly computer as though each had direct access. He later founded the Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, a key environment for experimental programming and early network culture.

In memoriam

McCarthy died in 2011. His insistence on expressive programming systems and shared interactive computing remains visible in modern research and hacker practice.
