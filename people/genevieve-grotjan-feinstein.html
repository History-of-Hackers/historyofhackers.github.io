Genevieve Grotjan Feinstein

Grotjan Feinstein's documented breakthroughs show the central role of mathematical analysis in cryptologic operations. Her record also preserves the contribution of a woman whose wartime work was largely classified during her career.

Genevieve Grotjan Feinstein was an American mathematician and cryptanalyst hired by the Army Signal Intelligence Service in 1939. The NSA records that her September 1940 breakthrough enabled the service to build an analog machine for work against Japan's Purple diplomatic system.

Cryptologic work

She later worked on the Soviet problem that became Venona. The NSA credits her with a process for identifying reused key material, an analytical step that assisted later decryption work.

In memoriam

Feinstein left government service in 1947 and later taught mathematics. She died in 2006 and was inducted into the NSA/CSS Cryptologic Hall of Honor in 2010.
