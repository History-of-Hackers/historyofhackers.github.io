Clifford Cocks

Cocks’s work illustrates an essential distinction in security history: a result can be independently discovered under classification without shaping the public research community until it is disclosed.

Clifford Cocks is a British mathematician and cryptographer. After research in number theory at Oxford, he joined the UK government cryptographic service in 1973.

Public-key work under classification

Responding to the challenge posed by James Ellis’s non-secret-encryption idea, Cocks developed a practical public-key encryption method in 1973. The work remained classified and was disclosed only in 1997; it therefore did not influence RSA’s independent public development.

Later recognition

The Royal Society credits Cocks with a 1973 practical public-key method and a 1998 identity-based-encryption discovery. He is included here for that carefully documented technical history rather than for a claim of sole priority.
