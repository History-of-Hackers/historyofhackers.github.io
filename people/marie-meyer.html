Marie Meyer

Meyer belongs in security history because cryptologic breakthroughs often relied on specialist language work that received far less public attention than machines or famous operations.

Marie Meyer was a language scholar with training in Latin and knowledge of several European languages. She joined the U.S. cryptologic service during World War II and later worked on the Soviet, or VENONA, problem.

Language work and VENONA

NSA historical material credits Meyer with some initial recoveries of the VENONA codebook and records that she taught Russian-language classes while continuing work on the Russian problem. This was analytical work in which linguistic evidence could guide cryptanalytic progress.

Legacy

Her career is an important record of the often-unseen linguists whose subject knowledge made signals intelligence usable.
