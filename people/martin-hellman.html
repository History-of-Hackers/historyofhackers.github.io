Martin Hellman

Hellman’s research helped make secure communication over open networks technically possible and academically legitimate.

Martin Hellman is a cryptographer and Stanford professor emeritus. With Whitfield Diffie, he co-authored the 1976 paper that opened a new direction for cryptography and introduced concepts central to public-key systems.

Open-network security

Hellman and Diffie focused on a practical challenge: how parties can communicate securely when they have not privately arranged a key in advance. Their ideas became fundamental to the cryptographic infrastructure behind secure digital communication.

Lasting impact

The work has remained influential in both research and deployed protocol design, and it was recognized with the 2015 ACM Turing Award.
