Dan Boneh

Boneh's work connects cryptographic research with practical computer-security problems. His teaching and research leadership have also made advanced cryptography more accessible to broad technical audiences.

Dan Boneh is a professor of computer science and electrical engineering at Stanford University. He heads Stanford's applied cryptography group and co-directs its Computer Security Lab.

Applied cryptography

His research focuses on applications of cryptography to computer security, including cryptosystems with novel properties, web security, mobile-device security, and cryptanalysis. Stanford records that he joined the faculty in 1997 after completing a Princeton Ph.D.

Research and teaching

Boneh has combined research with public educational materials in cryptography. The ACM recognized him with its 2014 Prize in Computing, and his work is frequently used to connect cryptographic theory with deployed systems.
