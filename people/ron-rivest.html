Ron Rivest

Rivest’s work helped establish public-key cryptography as a practical discipline, while his later work has engaged with the security of civic technology.

Ron Rivest is an MIT computer scientist and co-inventor of RSA public-key cryptography. RSA provided a practical construction for encryption and digital signatures based on a publicly shareable key, profoundly influencing computer security.

Cryptographic design

Rivest’s work spans algorithms, cryptographic primitives, and the societal uses of security technology. He has also contributed to discussions of verifiable elections and the risks of trusting opaque systems.

Recognition

Rivest shared the 2002 ACM Turing Award with Adi Shamir and Leonard Adleman for RSA.
