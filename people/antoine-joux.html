Antoine Joux

Joux's work shows both sides of public-key cryptography: bilinear pairings enabled new protocol constructions, while advances in discrete-logarithm algorithms clarify when the assumptions behind those constructions must be revisited.

Pairings and protocols

Antoine Joux is a French cryptographer whose research has addressed public-key cryptography, pairings and discrete logarithms. His work on a one-round tripartite Diffie–Hellman protocol is a landmark in the development of pairing-based cryptography, a family of techniques used in later identity-based and short-signature constructions.

Cryptanalytic research

Joux has also contributed to algorithms for discrete logarithms in finite fields. This dual role—constructing protocols while studying the assumptions that secure them—is central to responsible cryptographic research. His work has been recognized through the Gödel Prize for cryptographic advances.
