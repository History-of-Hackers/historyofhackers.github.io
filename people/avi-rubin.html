Avi Rubin

Rubin brought independent security evaluation of election technology into public view while pursuing academic work in applied cryptography, systems security, privacy, and health information security.

Aviel D. “Avi” Rubin is a professor emeritus of computer science at Johns Hopkins University and former technical director of its Information Security Institute. His research includes computer security, applied cryptography, network security, Web security, and secure Internet services.

Independent analysis of election systems

In 2003, Rubin and colleagues analyzed software believed to be used in Diebold electronic voting systems and publicly described serious security concerns. The work became a prominent example of independent scrutiny of technology used in civic infrastructure.

Research and public engagement

Rubin also worked in Bell-system research labs and has testified on security issues. His career illustrates how security research can inform engineering practice and public-policy debates.
