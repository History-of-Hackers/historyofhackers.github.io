Eva Galperin

Eva Galperin broadened cybersecurity practice by centering people whose principal threats are intimate partners, stalkers, or repressive governments rather than indiscriminate malware. Her work connected threat research, digital-rights advocacy, survivor support, and the antivirus industry.

Security for Vulnerable Populations

Eva Galperin is a security researcher and digital-rights advocate who has worked at the Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) since 2007 and serves as its Director of Cybersecurity. Before joining EFF, she worked in security and information technology in Silicon Valley and studied political science and international relations at San Francisco State University.

Galperin's work focuses on people for whom a compromised account or device may lead directly to arrest, harassment, stalking, or physical violence. At EFF she has contributed to security-training projects including Surveillance Self-Defense and the Digital First Aid Kit, and has investigated targeted malware campaigns affecting activists and journalists in countries including Syria, Vietnam, Lebanon, and Kazakhstan.

Targeted-Malware Research

Galperin combined malware analysis with political and regional context to document campaigns that could be missed by security programs focused on mass-market threats. In 2013 she co-authored a Citizen Lab and EFF report on surveillance malware aimed at Syrian opposition members. In 2018, EFF and Lookout published research into Dark Caracal, a mobile-surveillance campaign linked by the researchers to infrastructure associated with Lebanon's General Directorate of General Security. This work emphasized that modest tooling and social engineering can still produce serious consequences when campaigns are directed at exposed communities.

Stalkerware

Beginning in 2018, Galperin increasingly focused on technology-enabled intimate-partner abuse. Commercial surveillance applications marketed for monitoring employees, children, or partners could run covertly, collect messages and locations, and evade the clear malware labels that would normally warn a device owner. Galperin worked directly with survivors and pressed antivirus vendors to treat these applications as a distinct security threat.

In April 2019, Kaspersky introduced a clearer Privacy Alert for commercial spyware after discussions that the company and independent reporting credited in part to Galperin's advocacy. In November 2019, EFF joined antivirus companies and survivor-support organizations as one of ten founding partners of the Coalition Against Stalkerware; Galperin is identified by EFF as a co-founder. The coalition created shared definitions, training, sample exchange, and detection guidance so that security vendors and domestic-violence organizations could respond together.

Impact

Galperin's contribution is both technical and institutional. She helped establish survivor-centered security as a field in which removing spyware is not a purely technical act: discovery or deletion can alert an abuser and increase danger, so incident response must account for safety planning and the user's circumstances. By bringing those requirements into security-industry practice, her work expanded who threat models are designed to protect and what a successful security intervention should achieve.
