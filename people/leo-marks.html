Leo Marks

Marks improved field-cipher procedures for agents operating under extreme risk. His story makes the usability of security systems—not merely their mathematical strength—visible in the history of cryptography.

Leo Marks was the wartime head of the Special Operations Executive’s codes office. He trained and briefed agents and worked on communications procedures for clandestine operations in occupied Europe.

Human factors in cryptography

Marks is closely associated with replacing vulnerable poem-based keys with more secure procedures, including one-time-pad methods. His later memoir documented the practical constraints that shape whether a cryptographic system can be safely used in the field.

In memoriam

Marks died in 2001. He is remembered for emphasizing the operational realities behind secure communications.
