Whitfield Diffie

Diffie helped transform secure communication from a problem of secret shared keys into a public, mathematical field central to modern network security.

Whitfield Diffie is a cryptographer best known for the work that introduced the public-key distribution problem and the method now called Diffie–Hellman key exchange. The 1976 paper written with Martin Hellman changed how researchers thought about secure communication over open networks.

Changing the model

Before public-key cryptography, securely exchanging keys was a central obstacle. Diffie and Hellman described a way to establish a shared secret without first sharing one, opening a path toward many later cryptographic systems.

Security history

Their work is both a mathematical contribution and a cultural turning point: it made strong cryptography an openly discussed research subject rather than solely a government concern.
