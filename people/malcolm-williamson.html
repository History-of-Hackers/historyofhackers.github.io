Malcolm Williamson

Williamson’s work belongs in the history of public-key cryptography because it anticipated the central key-agreement idea independently, while remaining unavailable to the public field until GCHQ’s later disclosure.

Malcolm John Williamson was a British mathematician and cryptographer. At GCHQ in the 1970s, he worked with the conceptual framework introduced by James Ellis and developed an early key-agreement method.

A concealed parallel history

GCHQ and the NSA describe Williamson’s work alongside that of Ellis and Clifford Cocks as an early discovery of public-key cryptography. The history requires care: classified work did not circulate in the open community and did not displace the independent public work of Diffie, Hellman, Merkle, Rivest, Shamir, and Adleman.

In memoriam

Williamson died in 2015. His contribution was later formally recognised with his colleagues in the Cryptologic Hall of Honor.
