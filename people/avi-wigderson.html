Avi Wigderson

Avi Wigderson is included for documented contributions to the technical and formal foundations of computer security and cryptography.

Avi Wigderson is a professor at the Institute for Advanced Study whose research spans computational complexity, randomness, cryptography, and distributed computation. His work studies what can be computed efficiently and under what assumptions.

Security relevance

Modern cryptography depends on complexity assumptions, randomness, and adversarial models. Wigderson’s foundational research helps formalize these concepts and clarify the limits and possibilities of secure computation.

Timeline

  • 1980Receives a Ph.D. from Princeton University.
  • 1988Co-authors foundational work on fault-tolerant distributed computation.
  • 1999Joins the Institute for Advanced Study.
  • 2023Receives the ACM A.M. Turing Award for contributions to the theory of computation.
