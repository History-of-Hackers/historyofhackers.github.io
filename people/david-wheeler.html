David Wheeler

Wheeler's work links early stored-program computing, reusable software techniques, testing, and lightweight cryptographic design. His career at Cambridge documents how foundational programming practices were developed alongside computer engineering.

David John Wheeler was a British computer scientist associated with the University of Cambridge's Mathematical Laboratory and Computer Laboratory. He contributed to the EDSAC project and to techniques for structuring stored-program software.

Software and security work

Cambridge and the Computer History Museum credit Wheeler with the closed subroutine, a durable programming idea. Late in his career, he and Roger Needham designed the Tiny Encryption Algorithm, a compact block cipher published in the 1990s.

In memoriam

Wheeler remained active in Cambridge computing after formal retirement. He died in 2004; his work is remembered through the Wheeler Lecture and historical accounts of EDSAC-era programming.
