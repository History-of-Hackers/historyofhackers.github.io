Herman Hollerith

Hollerith’s systems made structured data processing operational at institutional scale. They are an early predecessor to the information systems whose access controls, privacy, and integrity are central security concerns today.

Herman Hollerith developed electromechanical tabulating equipment that used punched cards to process U.S. census data. His company later became part of the organization renamed International Business Machines.

Data at scale

The tabulating system accelerated large administrative calculations and established an enduring relationship between information processing, institutions, and technology.

In memoriam

Hollerith died in 1929. His inventions remain a key chapter in the pre-electronic history of information processing.
