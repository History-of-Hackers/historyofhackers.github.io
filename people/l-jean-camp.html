L. Jean Camp

Camp's work treats security as a socio-technical problem: people, incentives, interfaces, and institutions affect whether a technical defense can succeed. That perspective is essential to the history of usable security and security economics.

L. Jean Camp is an information-security researcher whose work addresses security economics, user-centered security, risk communication, online trust, privacy, and public policy. Her research joins technical security questions with the human and institutional settings in which systems operate.

Security as a human problem

Camp's academic work emphasizes that security depends on more than mechanisms: users need understandable choices, and organizations operate under incentives and constraints. Her work has applied these ideas to authentication, networking, Internet-of-Things systems, and online trust.

Research and teaching

Indiana University profiles describe her work across the intersection of human and technical trust, economic models, and human-centered design. This interdisciplinary approach makes her a significant figure in the development of usable-security research.
