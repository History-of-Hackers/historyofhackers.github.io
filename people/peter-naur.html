Peter Naur

Naur's work helped make programming-language specifications legible and reproducible. The notation associated with Backus and Naur became a durable tool for describing syntax, a prerequisite for reliable implementations and later language-security work.

Peter Naur was a Danish computer-science pioneer, initially trained in astronomy, who became a central contributor to programming-language design. He worked on the ALGOL 60 report and its influential syntactic notation.

Language description and theory

The ACM recognized Naur with the 2005 A.M. Turing Award for his role in defining ALGOL 60, including the pioneering use of a formal notation commonly called Backus-Naur form. He also wrote influential work on programming as theory building.

In memoriam

Naur died in 2016. His publications and bibliographic archive preserve a record of a career that joined programming-language practice with wider questions about human understanding of programs.
