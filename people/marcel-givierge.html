Marcel Givierge

Givierge helped turn cryptology into a trainable military specialty. His teaching and organizational work link earlier manual cryptanalysis to the institutional education of later codebreakers.

Marcel Givierge was a French Army officer and specialist in cryptology. During the First World War he worked in the French cipher service, and after the war he wrote instructional texts on cryptography.

Organization and teaching

Givierge’s work connected operational cryptanalysis with training. His Cours de cryptographie is part of the documentary record of how cipher methods were taught between the wars.

Historical importance

The development of cryptology depended not only on individual inventions but also on institutions able to teach, document, and sustain analytical methods. Givierge’s career exemplifies that quieter but essential infrastructure.

In memoriam

Givierge died in 1931. His publications and service record remain useful sources for the history of French military cryptology.
