Agnes Meyer Driscoll

Driscoll was a technical practitioner of naval cryptology across two world wars. Her career demonstrates the sustained analytical work behind cryptographic intelligence, including the often under-documented contributions of women in the field.

Agnes Meyer Driscoll was an American cryptanalyst associated with U.S. naval cryptology. Trained in mathematics and languages, she worked on naval codes and cipher systems from the First World War through the postwar cryptologic organizations.

Naval codebreaking

Driscoll worked on Japanese naval systems and trained or advised other cryptologic personnel. Her long career spanned the Navy's cryptologic work, the Armed Forces Security Agency, and the early National Security Agency era.

Historical record

Much operational detail remained classified for years; responsible accounts should avoid attributing every later naval success to one individual. The documented record nevertheless establishes her as a leading naval cryptanalyst and teacher.

In memoriam

Driscoll died in 1971. Her posthumous recognition has helped restore an important pioneer to the public history of cryptology.
