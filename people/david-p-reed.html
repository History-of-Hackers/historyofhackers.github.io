David P. Reed

Reed’s work articulated architectural principles that shaped the open internet, including where reliability and security functions belong in a networked system.

David P. Reed is an American computer scientist whose work spans computer networking, distributed systems, and internet architecture. He co-authored the end-to-end argument, a highly influential account of how functions should be placed in layered communication systems.

Architectural legacy

The end-to-end argument informed the design of internet protocols and remains a reference point in debates about security, reliability, privacy, and control in networked systems. Reed’s career also included work on early network protocols and interactive computing.
