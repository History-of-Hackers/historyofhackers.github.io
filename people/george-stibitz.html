George Stibitz

Stibitz’s relay calculators showed that telephone relays could perform binary arithmetic, and his 1940 demonstration is an early, well-documented instance of remote computer access.

George Stibitz worked at Bell Laboratories on relay-based calculation. His home-built “Model K” demonstrated binary addition; Bell Labs later developed the Complex Number Calculator from related relay techniques.

Remote operation

At an American Mathematical Society meeting in 1940, attendees used a Teletype connection to operate the Complex Number Calculator in New York from Dartmouth. The event is a significant early example of remote computing.

In memoriam

Stibitz died in 1995. His papers and artifacts continue to support research on the formative period of digital computing.
