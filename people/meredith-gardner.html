Meredith Gardner

Gardner’s work illustrates how linguistic expertise and cryptanalytic method were jointly necessary to interpret high-value encrypted communications.

Meredith Gardner was a linguist recruited to the U.S. Army Signals Intelligence Service in 1942. After wartime language and cryptologic work, he moved to the Soviet problem and became a leading analyst on VENONA.

VENONA

VENONA was the long-running effort to exploit Soviet diplomatic and intelligence messages. NSA historical material describes Gardner as an early principal analyst; his 1946 work opened a path to reading messages made vulnerable by one-time-pad key reuse.

In memoriam

Gardner died in 2002. His record is a reminder that cryptanalysis depends on language, context, and careful interpretation as well as mathematics.
