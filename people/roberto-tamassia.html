Roberto Tamassia

Tamassia's work on authentication and secure data structures makes a useful bridge between cryptographic ideas and systems that must prove the integrity and origin of distributed data.

Roberto Tamassia is a computer scientist at Brown University whose research includes information security, applied cryptography, algorithms, and data structures. His security work has focused in part on authentication methods and protocols.

Authentication in distributed systems

Security is not only about keeping data secret: systems must also establish integrity, provenance, and correct access. Tamassia's research has developed cryptographic techniques and prototypes for authenticating data in distributed settings.

Teaching and research

His work also reflects the role of computer-security education in the field. Brown records that he developed an early-access computer systems security course and has worked across security, cryptography, algorithms, and theory.
