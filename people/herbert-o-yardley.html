Herbert O. Yardley

Yardley’s career documents the formative institutional period of U.S. cryptanalysis and the lasting tension between secrecy, disclosure, and communications security.

Herbert O. Yardley was an American cryptanalyst who served with the U.S. Army’s cryptologic effort in the First World War and led the postwar Cipher Bureau, commonly called the Black Chamber. The organization analyzed foreign diplomatic ciphers during the 1920s.

Disclosure and legacy

After the Bureau closed, Yardley published The American Black Chamber in 1931. NSA historical material treats the book and its repercussions as an important episode in the history of American cryptology and communications security.

In memoriam

Yardley died in 1958. His work remains central to the institutional history of U.S. cryptanalysis.
