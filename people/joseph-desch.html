Joseph Desch

Desch translated cryptanalytic requirements into a rapidly manufactured electromechanical system. His work demonstrates that security history is also a history of systems engineering under urgent operational constraints.

Joseph Desch was an electrical engineer at the National Cash Register Company who became the principal engineer for the U.S. Navy’s four-rotor Bombe project during the Second World War. The machines were built to support analysis of German naval Enigma traffic.

Engineering for cryptanalysis

Desch’s team converted complex cryptanalytic requirements into practical, high-speed machines under a compressed schedule. The resulting Bombes helped process the settings needed to read protected communications in the U-boat campaign.

In memoriam

Desch died in 1987. His work is preserved as a major example of the engineering effort behind Allied cryptanalysis.
