Timeline

Explicitly dated milestones drawn from sourced entries across the archive, oldest first. Only dates stated in an entry's own sources appear here — nothing is estimated or invented.

  • 1956-09-15Born in Glasgow, Scotland. — Ross Anderson
  • 1959Term 'hack' documented in MIT student groups' vocabulary, including TMRC. — The Hacker Ethic: MIT's Tech Model Railroad Club
  • 1963-01-15Born in New York City. — Bruce Schneier
  • 1963-08-06Born in Los Angeles, California. — Kevin Mitnick: A Complete History
  • 1964-10-23Born in Nagoya, Japan. — Tsutomu Shimomura
  • 1965-11-08Born in the United States, son of Bell Labs/NSA computer scientist Robert Morris. — Robert Tappan Morris
  • 1969-10-13Born in Finland. — Mikko Hyppönen
  • 1970-12-01Born in Alabama. — Peiter "Mudge" Zatko
  • 1977Alexander Peslyak is born. — Solar Designer
  • 1977-11-22Born in Auckland, New Zealand. — Barnaby Jack
  • 1979-02-07Born in San Francisco, California. — Dan Kaminsky
  • 1981CCC founded in Berlin. — The Chaos Computer Club (CCC)
  • 1981-01-19Born in Poland. — Michal Zalewski
  • 1981-08-11Born in Germany. — Karsten Nohl
  • 1982-06-02Born in Ford City, Pennsylvania. — Chris Valasek
  • 1983Announces the GNU Project. — Richard Stallman, the GNU Project, and Free Software
  • 1984The 'BTX-Hack' publicly demonstrates a flaw in Germany's Bildschirmtext system. — The Chaos Computer Club (CCC)
  • 1984cDc founded in Lubbock, Texas. — The Cult of the Dead Cow (cDc) and Hacktivism
  • 1984Legion of Doom founded by Lex Luthor. — Legion of Doom (LOD): The Masters of the Digital Universe
  • 1984Steven Levy publishes 'Hackers: Heroes of the Computer Revolution', codifying the Hacker Ethic. — The Hacker Ethic: MIT's Tech Model Railroad Club
  • 19842600: The Hacker Quarterly founded by Eric Corley ('Emmanuel Goldstein'). — 2600: The Hacker Quarterly
  • 1985-11-17First issue of Phrack released. — Phrack Magazine: The Hacker's Ezine
  • 1985-12-10Samy Kamkar is born. — Samy Kamkar
  • 1986Begins investigating a 75-cent accounting discrepancy at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. — Clifford Stoll and 'The Cuckoo's Egg'
  • 1986Published in Phrack under the handle 'The Mentor'. — The Hacker Manifesto: 'The Conscience of a Hacker'
  • 1988Convicted for the DEC intrusion: 12 months prison, 3 years supervised release. — Kevin Mitnick: A Complete History
  • 1988As a Cornell graduate student, releases a self-replicating program (the Morris Worm) from MIT; a bug causes it to spread uncontrollably. — Robert Tappan Morris
  • 1988-11-02Morris Worm released, infects an estimated 10% of the ~60,000 hosts then on the internet. — The Morris Worm: The Internet's First Wake-Up Call
  • 1989Publishes 'The Cuckoo's Egg'. — Clifford Stoll and 'The Cuckoo's Egg'
  • 1989Joined UC San Diego's physics department and became a senior fellow at the San Diego Supercomputer Center. — Tsutomu Shimomura
  • 1990-12Sentenced to three years probation, 400 hours community service, and a $10,050 fine as the first person convicted under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act. — Robert Tappan Morris
  • 1991Joins Data Fellows (later F-Secure) as a security researcher. — Mikko Hyppönen
  • 1992Testified before the U.S. Congress on cellular telephone security and privacy vulnerabilities. — Tsutomu Shimomura
  • 1992Key MOD members arrested in a federal crackdown. — Masters of Deception: The New York Hacker Wars
  • 1993Designed the Blowfish symmetric-key block cipher as a free, unpatented alternative to DES. — Bruce Schneier
  • 1993First DEF CON held in Las Vegas, organized by Jeff Moss ('The Dark Tangent'). — DEF CON: The World's Most Famous Hacker Con
  • 1994Published Applied Cryptography, which became a standard reference text in the field. — Bruce Schneier
  • 1994Adopts the handle "Solar Designer" to register on private BBSes. — Solar Designer
  • 1994-12-25An intruder (later identified as Kevin Mitnick) launched an IP-spoofing attack against Shimomura's home computer systems. — Tsutomu Shimomura
  • 1995Publishes "How to Write Buffer Overflows," an early technical paper on buffer-overflow exploitation. — Peiter "Mudge" Zatko
  • 1995Co-founds Viaweb with Paul Graham. — Robert Tappan Morris
  • 1995Awarded PhD at Cambridge; becomes University Lecturer at the Cambridge Computer Laboratory. — Ross Anderson
  • 1995-01-23CERT/CC issued Advisory CA-1995-01 on IP spoofing, around the same time as New York Times coverage of the break-in. — Tsutomu Shimomura
  • 1995-02-15Arrested by the FBI in Raleigh, North Carolina. — Kevin Mitnick: A Complete History
  • 1995-02-15FBI agents arrested Kevin Mitnick in Raleigh, North Carolina, with Shimomura having assisted in tracking him. — Tsutomu Shimomura
  • 1996Releases John the Ripper, a password-cracking tool for Unix systems. — Solar Designer
  • 1996cDc coins the term 'hacktivism'. — The Cult of the Dead Cow (cDc) and Hacktivism
  • 1996First released by Solar Designer (Alexander Peslyak). — John the Ripper: The Timeless Password Cracker
  • 1996-01Shimomura and John Markoff published 'Takedown.' — Tsutomu Shimomura
  • 1997Publishes early return-into-libc buffer overflow exploits and a Linux kernel non-executable-memory hardening patch. — Solar Designer
  • 1997Nmap source code first published in Phrack Magazine, issue 51. — Nmap: The Network Mapper
  • 1998Co-designed the Twofish block cipher, selected as one of five finalists in NIST's AES competition. — Bruce Schneier
  • 1998Began publishing the Crypto-Gram newsletter. — Bruce Schneier
  • 1998Yahoo! acquires Viaweb for about $49 million; relaunched as Yahoo! Store. — Robert Tappan Morris
  • 1998Back Orifice released at DEF CON 6. — The Cult of the Dead Cow (cDc) and Hacktivism
  • 1998First release, under the name Ethereal. — Wireshark: Sniffing the Wires

  • 1998-05-19Testifies with six other L0pht members before the U.S. Senate Committee on Governmental Affairs. — Peiter "Mudge" Zatko

  • 1999Co-founded Counterpane Internet Security. — Bruce Schneier
  • 1999Earns a PhD from Harvard University. — Robert Tappan Morris
  • 1999Founds and leads the Openwall Project. — Solar Designer
  • 2000Released; re-enters the security industry as a consultant. — Kevin Mitnick: A Complete History
  • 2000-06-10Releases p0f, a passive OS/network fingerprinting tool, announced on Bugtraq. — Michal Zalewski
  • 2001Publishes the first edition of 'Security Engineering' and the paper 'Why Information Security is Hard'. — Ross Anderson

  • 2002Joined penetration-testing firm @stake as a security researcher. — Katie Moussouris
  • 2002Co-founds the Workshop on the Economics of Information Security (WEIS). — Ross Anderson
  • 2002-02-19Publicly disclosed a buffer overflow in Microsoft SQL Server 7.0/2000's openrowset and opendatasource functions. — Cesar Cerrudo

  • 2003Founds Openwall, Inc. and becomes its CTO. — Solar Designer

  • 2003Becomes Professor of Security Engineering at the University of Cambridge. — Ross Anderson

  • 2004Founds 'News from the Lab,' an early antivirus-industry research blog. — Mikko Hyppönen
