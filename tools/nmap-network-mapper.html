Nmap: The Network Mapper

The story of Nmap, the essential open-source utility for network discovery and security auditing, created by Gordon 'Fyodor' Lyon.

In 1997, Gordon Lyon, writing under the pseudonym Fyodor Vaskovich, published the source code for a new tool in Phrack Magazine: the Network Mapper, or Nmap. What started as a relatively simple port scanner quickly evolved into the de facto industry standard for network discovery and security auditing.

Nmap's power lies in its versatility. It can discover hosts on a network, identify open ports, detect the services (and their versions) running on those ports, and even determine the operating system of the target machine. Its scripting engine (NSE) allows users to automate a wide variety of networking tasks, from advanced vulnerability detection to complex service discovery.

Written in C++ and available on all major operating systems, Nmap is a testament to the power of open-source development. It has been featured in numerous films, including The Matrix Reloaded and Die Hard 4, solidifying its place in both real-world security and popular culture.
