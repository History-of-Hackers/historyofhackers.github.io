Cesar Cerrudo

Person · 2000s-Present

Cesar Cerrudo is an Argentine security researcher known for early vulnerability research in Microsoft SQL Server and Windows, and for later work as CTO of IOActive Labs exposing widespread security weaknesses in traffic-control systems and other smart-city infrastructure.

James Kettle

Person · 2010s-Present

James Kettle, known online as 'albinowax', is a British web security researcher and Director of Research at PortSwigger (maker of Burp Suite), best known for reviving and popularizing HTTP request smuggling through his 2019 'HTTP Desync Attacks' research, alongside influential work on web cache poisoning and web race conditions.

Karsten Nohl

Person · 2000s-Present

Karsten Nohl is a German cryptographer and security researcher best known for cracking the GSM A5/1 cipher and the MIFARE Classic RFID card encryption, and for co-discovering the 2014 BadUSB firmware vulnerability; he leads Security Research Labs, a Berlin-based security research consultancy.

Michal Zalewski

Person · 1990s-Present

Polish-born security researcher known online as 'lcamtuf', a prolific Bugtraq-era vulnerability researcher whose passive-fingerprinting tool p0f, web-security books, and coverage-guided fuzzer American Fuzzy Lop (afl) reshaped both browser-security research and modern bug-finding practice.

Mikko Hyppönen

Person · 1990s-Present

Mikko Hyppönen is a Finnish computer security researcher who spent over three decades at F-Secure and its successor WithSecure, becoming one of the most publicly recognized voices in malware research and cybersecurity communication, known for tracking down early virus authors and for widely viewed talks on cybercrime.

Ross Anderson

Person · 1980s-2024

Ross Anderson was a British computer scientist and Professor of Security Engineering at the University of Cambridge whose research on cryptography, banking and ATM fraud, and the economics of information security helped establish security engineering as a recognized academic discipline, and whose 2001 textbook 'Security Engineering' became a standard reference in the field.
