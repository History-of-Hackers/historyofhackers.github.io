Marc Maiffret

Person · 1990s-Present

Marc Maiffret is an American security researcher who, as a teenage former hacker, co-founded eEye Digital Security in 1998 and went on to discover and publicly analyze the 2001 Code Red worm, becoming an early and influential figure in commercial Windows/IIS vulnerability research.

Eric Corley

Person · 1980s-Present

Eric Corley, known publicly by the pen name Emmanuel Goldstein, is an American publisher and broadcaster whose radio program, biennial HOPE hacker conference, and role as lead defendant in the DeCSS anti-circumvention lawsuit made him one of the most visible individual figures in American hacker culture from the 1980s onward.

Gordon Lyon

Person · 1990s-Present

Gordon Lyon, known by the handle 'Fyodor,' wrote and released the Nmap Security Scanner in 1997 and has remained its lead developer for nearly three decades, while also founding Insecure.Org, SecLists.Org, and SecTools.Org — long-running pieces of infrastructure that the security research community still relies on.

John Draper

Person · 1970s-Present

John Draper, known as 'Captain Crunch,' is a foundational figure of phone phreaking whose discovery that a cereal-box toy whistle reproduced AT&T's 2600 Hz control tone helped launch blue-box phreaking and, indirectly, informed the founding partnership between Steve Wozniak and Steve Jobs.

Kevin Poulsen

Person · 1980s-Present

Kevin Poulsen, known by the handle 'Dark Dante,' was a phone phreaker and hacker who rigged a 1990 radio-station phone contest to win a Porsche and later reinvented himself as an award-winning Wired News journalist who helped uncover Chelsea Manning as the source of the WikiLeaks military and diplomatic leaks.

the Grugq

Person · 2000s-Present

The Grugq is a pseudonymous information-security researcher, reportedly of South African origin, best known for pioneering early anti-forensics research in the 2000s, later reported involvement as a broker of zero-day exploits, and an ongoing career as an influential public commentator on operational security (OPSEC) and cyber conflict.
